cEDH guide

Competitive EDH (cEDH) is Commander played to win: optimized 100-card singleton decks, four-player pods, and games that routinely end in a few turns. This guide covers the metagame, combos, staples, and the vocabulary. Metagame numbers are a dated snapshot; card legality is checked live against Scryfall.

What makes cEDH different

Everyone is trying to win early

Decks run fast mana, cheap tutors, and compact win conditions, so the question each turn is “can someone win now, and can I stop it?”

Interaction is the game

Counterspells, free spells, and removal are held for the moment someone tries to win. Most games are decided by who has an answer ready, not who has the biggest board.

The commander is a tool

It is a repeatable engine, tutor, or mana source you can always access. Many top commanders are cheap partners that simply let you pick your colors.

Politics are lighter than casual

You still talk, but threats are judged mostly on who is closest to winning. Pods tend to gang up on whoever has just resolved a game-ending engine.

How a typical game plays out

  1. Mulligans. Keep hands with a land or fast mana, a way to dig or tutor, and ideally one piece of protection. A slow “fair” hand is usually a mulligan.
  2. Turns 1–2: accelerate and set up. Mana rocks, rituals, and cheap engines. Holding up free counterspells is common.
  3. Turns 3–5: the windows. Someone assembles a win (often a combo) while the rest of the table decides whether to stop it. Pass priority carefully and watch the stack.
  4. Protect your win. A win attempt that can be countered or answered is just a bluff. Keep protection up if you can.

Tips for your first pod

  • Play the same four-person pod a few times. Learning what each deck does matters more than raw deck power.
  • Announce what you are doing step by step; unclear stack actions cause most disputes.
  • Agree beforehand on rules for tournaments or casual play: proxies, turn timers, and draw rules. Sanctioned events differ from kitchen-table pods.
  • Start from a known list from the cEDH Decklist Database rather than building from scratch.

Metagame snapshot

Entries show how often a commander is played; PPG (points per game) shows how well it does: 5 points for a win, 1 for a draw, 0 for a loss, divided by games. A neutral deck sits near the baseline marked on each bar. Tiers below are drawn from PPG relative to that baseline.

Read these numbers carefully. PPG is not adjusted for player skill, pod makeup, or sample size, so a commander with fewer entries can look better or worse than it really is. Popular commanders attract many different pilots, which pulls PPG toward the baseline. Use tiers as a guide to what you will face, not a verdict on which deck to buy.

The main ways cEDH decks win

Turbo / combo

Maximum fast mana and tutors to assemble a win on turn 1–3. Strengths: speed. Weaknesses: needs the opening hand to work, folds to early disruption.

Midrange / “good stuff”

Strong card advantage engines plus a couple of combo lines. The pod favourite for flexibility: it can disrupt, grind, or go for the win depending on the table. Many blue-based partner decks play this way.

Spellslinger / storm

Chains many cheap spells to build resources, then ends the game with a storm-style finisher. Strong with rituals and free spells; punished by hard counters and graveyard hate.

Stax

Taxes or shuts off resources (mana, lands, spells) so opponents cannot function while you keep an engine going. Slower, and many tables hate it. Strong against decks that need to set up.

Creature combo / value

Wins with creature engines such as infinite mana plus an outlet, or ETB loops. Vulnerable to sweepers and removal but hard for blue decks to stop with only counterspells.

Voltron / aggro

Wins through combat damage with one protected threat. Rare at the top tables, but it dodges combo hate.

Choosing a deck

  • Want to learn the format? A midrange partner deck teaches the most: every important card type shows up.
  • Like fast, decisive games? Turbo/combo, but expect mulligans and misplays to cost games.
  • Like interaction puzzles? Spellslinger or blue-based midrange.
  • Strict budget? Many lists lean on expensive fast mana and dual lands, so check prices before committing; the decklist database shows budget-friendly options for some commanders.

Banlist and what it means for cEDH

  • The official Commander banlist is maintained by Wizards of the Coast and changes a few times a year. Check the official page before building.
  • September 2024 was a landmark: Mana Crypt, Jeweled Lotus, Dockside Extortionist, and Nadu, Winged Wisdom were banned, which removed some of the format’s fastest mana and engines.
  • Bans tend to hit the most consistent turbo cards, so older metagame articles can name cards that are no longer legal. The staples and combos tabs flag any banned card live, but still double-check older decklists.
  • Tournament organizers may add their own rules, such as proxy policies or custom banlists. Read the event page.

Brackets

Wizards’ Commander Brackets rank decks from 1 to 5. Bracket 5 is cEDH: decks built for maximum competitive power, where the Game Changers list and other restrictions of lower brackets no longer apply. If you are not sure whether a table wants cEDH, ask first and tell them which bracket your deck is.

Glossary

Fast mana
Permanents or spells that give more mana than they cost: Sol Ring, Mana Vault, Lotus Petal, Chrome Mox.
Free spell
A spell you can cast without paying its mana cost, usually by exiling a card or paying life: Force of Will, Fierce Guardianship, Deflecting Swat.
Tutor
A card that searches your library for another card.
Win-con
The way a deck ends the game: a combo, an infinite loop, combat damage, or a mill-out.
Combo
A group of cards that together do something game-ending, usually a loop that repeats infinitely.
Stack
The zone where spells and abilities wait to resolve. Interaction happens here.
Pod
The four players at a table.
Partner
A keyword that lets you have two commanders. Many top cEDH commanders are partners, which gives access to more colors.
PPG
Points per game, a tournament-based measure of how well a commander performs; see the meta tab.
Stax
A strategy that taxes or locks opponents’ resources.
Storm
A mechanic and strategy that rewards casting many spells in a turn.
Turbo
Aggressively fast combo decks that try to win before opponents can set up.

Mana Guide

Dual lands by cycle and color pair, plus rules of thumb for building a Commander mana base. Land lists are modeled on the Mana Gathering dual lands guide.

How many lands?

  • 36–38 lands is the usual range for a 99-card Commander deck, about 37% of the deck.
  • Run toward 34–35 with 10 or more ramp and mana rocks, a low curve, or a cheap commander. Run 38–40 with a high curve, big X spells, or little ramp.
  • Count mana rocks and ramp spells that find lands as part of your mana, not as extra lands.

How many sources of each color?

Count the lands (and mana rocks) that make each color. These are rules of thumb, not exact odds:

Role of the colorSources to aim for
Main color, with many early spellsAbout 20 or more
Second colorAbout 15–18
Third colorAbout 12–14
Splash of a few cardsAbout 8–10, more if you need it early

Colored costs like {W}{W} or {U}{U}{U} need more sources than a single pip, and cheap spells need them sooner. Run the Deck Analyzer tab to see your pip counts and sources by color.

Count your pips

Add up the colored mana symbols in every nonland card. The color with the most pips usually gets the most sources and the most basics. Do the count again after each swap.

Kinds of fixing

  • Untapped for free: original duals, and in some decks the Command Tower style lands. Best, usually expensive or restricted.
  • Untapped with a condition: shock lands (2 life), fast lands (early game), check lands (need a matching basic type), pain lands (damage when tapped for color).
  • Tapped with a perk: temples (scry), surveil lands, gain lands, bounce lands (they return a land, so they act like two).
  • Slow but cheap: gates, gain lands, slow lands and filter lands. Fine in slower decks, but too many tapped lands will hurt in faster pods.
  • Three colors at once: triomes enter tapped and have basic land types, so fetch lands and Nature's Lore effects can find them.

Triomes

Quick tips

  • Keep your basics. They matter for Farseek, Cultivate, check lands and slow lands.
  • Limit tapped lands, with a few more in decks that win slowly.
  • Add colorless-only utility lands only after your colors are stable, since each one costs you a colored source.
  • Check what your pod will bear: some groups ban or avoid the original duals and the most expensive lands.

Universes Beyond and sets with no story

Universes Beyond sets are crossovers with other franchises. They are not part of Magic's own multiverse story. Core sets and reprint sets (for example Foundations, 2024) also carry little or no continuing story.

    The Low Sodium rules

    Low Sodium is a Commander house format built to keep multiplayer games free of the play patterns that tend to sour a table. Rather than capping power level, it bans specific kinds of effects — and every card that does them — so whatever is left can be played freely. Decks can still range from slow and silly to fast and focused.

    What's banned

    Tap a rule to jump to the cards it bans. Counts are cards on the list.

      How the two lists work

      • The 99. A card on the deck ban list can't be played anywhere — not in the 99 and not as your commander.
      • The command zone. Commanders face a longer, stricter list, because you always have access to them. A card banned only as a commander is still legal in the 99.
      • Game Changers. Every Wizards-designated Game Changer is banned, whichever rule catches it.
      The “locks” and “severe resource imbalances” rules involve judgment calls — they exist to catch the most one-sided engines (think Rhystic Study or Smothering Tithe) and game-ending locks (think Avacyn, Angel of Hope or Constant Mists), so expect the exact wording to be broader than it looks.

      Commander game table

      A life and counter tracker built for the table. Put your phone or tablet flat in the middle, and each player gets their own card, turned to face their seat. Tap the left side of a card to lose life and the right side to gain it; hold for 10.

      Game in progress

      How it works

      • Life: tap the left or right half of a card, or hold for 10. The “…” button opens that player's counters.
      • Commander damage: tracked per opponent, with partner support. Damage over 20 from one commander defeats a player, and it also lowers life if you leave that option on.
      • Counters: poison (10 is lethal), energy, experience, rad, commander tax, your own custom counters, monarch, initiative, and the city's blessing.
      • Center button: dice and coins, turn order, mana pool, game log with undo, and game settings. Your game is saved automatically.
      • Commander art: in a player's menu, type a commander to use its art as that player's card background.
